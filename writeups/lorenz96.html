Technical Deep Dive

Parameterization of Lorenz-96 and Testing Online Stability Against Training Data Volume

Author: Sumangalam Avtar Sharma Affiliation: Department of Mathematics, IISER Pune Timeline: Aug 2026 - Present

1. Mathematical Formulation

Atmospheric circulation models necessarily parameterize subgrid-scale physical phenomena due to computational limits. The two-tier multi-scale Lorenz-96 equations offer a classical testbed for studying turbulence closures:

$$\frac{dX_k}{dt} = -X_{k-1}(X_{k-2} - X_{k+1}) - X_k + F - U_k(X, Y)$$

where $k = 1, \dots, K$ designates resolved slow synoptic variables with cyclic boundary conditions ($X_{k+K} = X_k$), $F$ represents constant external forcing, and the subgrid tendency $U_k$ is governed by coupling to fast micro-scale variables $Y_{j,k}$:

$$U_k(X, Y) = \frac{hc}{b} \sum_{j=1}^{J} Y_{j,k}$$

When reducing the equations to resolved dynamics only, one trains a statistical or machine learning parameterization $\hat{U}_k(X) \approx U_k$. While regression metrics ($R^2$, MSE) quantify offline fit, they routinely fail to capture non-linear feedbacks once $\hat{U}_k$ is coupled back online into the differential equations.

2. Research Objective

Core Research Question: What minimal training-data volume is required for a neural network parameterization of the Lorenz-96 subgrid coupling term $U_k$ to remain stable and physically consistent when coupled online into the reduced model, as opposed to merely being accurate offline?

3. Computational Protocol

Adapting methodologies from $M^2LInES$, we established a structured training and evaluation pipeline:

  • Data Volume Spectrum: Integrations swept over 10 separate sample sizes logarithmically distributed across simulation length.
  • Activation Regimes: Evaluated two distinct activation function profiles to analyze differentiability and spectral leakage effects.
  • Ensemble Construction: 20 independently trained neural models benchmarked against the full two-layer truth.
  • Online Verification: Long integrations ($t > 10,000$ steps) tracking kinetic energy conservation, autocorrelation functions, and probability density functions of the state variables.
